ICT Applications

From newsletters to satellite navigation, this is the big one: a tour of where ICT actually shows up in the real world — communication media, modelling, robotics, school systems, booking systems, banking, medicine, expert systems, retail, recognition systems, and satellites.

Communication media

Communication media describes the tools and channels for sharing information between a sender and a receiver. It can be both electronic and physical.

Newsletters

Periodic publications, often used to inform, educate, or entertain subscribers about specific topics. Usually distributed through email or as printed copies, and suitable for organisations, schools and clubs to keep members up to date.

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Cost effective; measurable results; easy to personalise; targeted; increases brand awarenessLow engagement; SPAM filters can unintentionally block them; limited interactivity; can get lost in a busy inbox

Posters

Visual presentations that convey information or promote events or products, combining text, images and graphics to grab attention.

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Cost effective; wide reach; versatile; long shelf life in the right spaceLimited information; lack of interactivity; can be removed or defaced; cannot be updated

Websites

Online platforms that provide information or services, accessible through the internet using a web browser. Useful for businesses, educational institutions and individuals to share information, sell products, or offer services.

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Media rich; links to other websites; easy navigation; global audienceCan be hacked; risk of pharming; maintenance can be expensive

Multimedia presentations

Digital presentations that combine various forms of media, such as text, images, audio and video — used in education, business and entertainment to present information in a visually engaging way.

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Media rich; interactive; transitions/animations to highlight key informationSpecial equipment required; often needs internet access; easy to create a bad presentation; visuals can take focus from the overall message

Audio & video

Audio is sound recordings such as podcasts, music and radio shows, which can be streamed or downloaded for offline listening — suitable for information, entertainment or education. Video is moving images with or without sound, used for entertainment, education or promotional purposes; typical platforms include YouTube, Vimeo and social media websites.

Media streaming

The real-time transmission of audio and video files over the internet, allowing users to access content without downloading it to their device. Popular services include Spotify, Netflix and YouTube.

E-Publications

Digital versions of printed materials, such as eBooks, eMagazines and eNewspapers, read on electronic devices such as eReaders, tablets and smartphones. They offer benefits like portability, searchability, and adjustable text size.

Computer modelling

Computer modelling is using a computer to simulate a real-life system in order to study its behaviour in different scenarios. The simulation uses a model to help predict how the system will work in unusual circumstances. Examples of modelling applications include personal finance, bridge and building design, flood water management, traffic management, and weather forecasting.

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Cheaper than building the real thing; easier to try a range of scenarios; safer — can simulate hazardous environments, e.g. a power station; faster — can speed up variables that would impact results, e.g. climate change; testing can be done before real-life construction beginsA computer model will never be 100% accurate; results are only as good as the model designed and data entered; possible human error in the design could skew results

Modelling applications

  • Personal finance — using a spreadsheet to test "what if" scenarios, e.g. the financial impact on a family if household income were to go down
  • Bridge and building design — architects use 3D computer models to test scenarios: for bridges, how much traffic it can take, the effects of natural vibrations and different weather conditions, and what impact an accident would have on structural integrity; for buildings, the effects of weather, simulating fire evacuation protocols, and what impact a fire would have on structural integrity
  • Flood water management — computer models allow risk assessments of high-risk flooding areas, using variables like different rivers/sea inlets, time of day, previous flood behaviours and possible obstructions; an automated system allows real-time monitoring, data collection and faster response to changing conditions, e.g. advance warning to people
  • Traffic management — computer models let users see the effect of traffic and accidents on major road networks, using data such as time of day, number of vehicles passing a junction, and factors like pedestrians crossing or accidents, to optimise traffic light timings; once modelling is successful, optimised systems can be built and installed
  • Weather forecasting — sensors collect data such as air pressure, wind speed, air temperature and humidity and send it to a central computer; the model predicts weather patterns for the next few days, and actual weather is compared to the modelled data to check accuracy and identify areas of the model to modify

School management systems

A school management system is a software solution designed to centralise and streamline a school's administrative tasks. Data is stored centrally to improve efficiency. Processes it can include: registration & attendance, recording learner performance, and computer aided learning (CAL).

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Streamlined administration and record-keeping; improved communication between teachers, students and parents (which can be automated); centralised access to information and resources; data-driven decision-making and insights for teachers and administrators; information is more up to date; information can be obtained quickly in an emergency; patterns of absence can be found quickly, helping to tackle truancy/latenessCost; transition can be time consuming and complex; training is required; dependent on technology; data security concerns

Registration & attendance

Traditional ways of recording attendance involve a teacher manually inputting data. IT systems make other methods possible, for example magnetic stripe cards and/or biometrics.

Recording learner performance

IT systems give teachers a plethora of information which can be analysed to identify class and individual strengths and areas for development. Data can be used to inform teaching strategies, provide individual progress reports, and critically analyse class/teacher performance.

Computer aided learning (CAL)

CAL is designed to enhance, not replace, traditional classroom teaching. It allows students to work on individual development areas, and can provide interactive revision resources to aid exam performance.

Online booking systems

An online booking system is a computer system that allows customers to purchase tickets for events online. Examples of industries that use these systems:

  • Travel — booking flights, hotels and package holidays
  • Music — reserving tickets for live music events
  • Film — booking cinema tickets, allowing customers to pick a seat
  • Sport — purchasing tickets to sporting events, choosing a view
AdvantagesDisadvantages
Convenience and accessibility (24/7 booking); instant confirmation and ticketing; ability to compare prices and options; promotions and personalised offers; faster to change/cancel; automated reminders via email/text; repeated bookings can easily be made; staff are freed up to do other thingsPotential for technical issues and downtime; possible security and privacy concerns; transaction and booking fees; impersonal and less tailored customer service; internet-connected devices required; staff may lose their job

Case study: reserving a cinema seat

After the user logs in, the following process takes place:

  1. Customer selects the film they want to watch
  2. Customer chooses a date
  3. The database is searched, and times and availability are displayed on the screen
  4. Customer chooses a suitable time
  5. The seating chart is displayed, showing available/taken seats
  6. Customer clicks on an available seat
  7. Total price is displayed on the screen
  8. Customer can choose another seat, or click confirm to continue to payment
  9. Seat(s) are temporarily made unavailable for booking by any other user
  10. Customer checks their personal details are correct and enters payment information
  11. Payment is taken and a confirmation message is displayed
  12. Payment receipt and QR code (e-ticket) are emailed
  13. The database is updated to make the seats unavailable

Banking applications

ATM

An Automatic Teller Machine (ATM) provides customers with convenient access to financial transactions 24/7, including: withdrawing cash, depositing cash or cheques, checking account balance, mini statements, bill paying, and money transfers.

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Convenient access to banking services; available 24/7; reduced waiting time; global access to fundsCash withdrawal limits; risk of theft or fraud; limited services compared to bank branches; technical issues and machine downtime; some banks charge for using ATMs

EFT and EFTPOS

Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT) is a way of moving money from one account to another, used primarily for salary deposits, bill payments, and online purchases.

Electronic Funds Transfer at the Point of Sale (EFTPOS) is used by retailers to allow customers to make purchases using a debit or credit card. The process involves:

  • Data is read from the chip (using RFID/NFC if it's a contactless payment)
  • The business's bank computer contacts the customer's bank computer
  • The card is checked to see if it is valid
  • If valid, the transaction continues; if not, it is terminated
  • An authorisation code is sent to the business
  • The price of the item is deducted from the customer's account
  • This money is added to the business's account
AdvantagesDisadvantages
Fast and efficient; reduces paperwork; lower transaction costs; EFT transactions can be disputed for up to 60 daysRisk of online fraud; technical issues; requires an internet connection; requires immediate funds available

Internet banking

Internet banking is a secure online platform for accessing and controlling a person's bank account, accessed via a secure website or a bank's official app. It allows transfers, bill payments, account management and more.

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Convenience and 24/7 access; easy account management; reduced need for branch visits (saving time and money); interest rates may be better; easier to shop around for the best accountSecurity of transactions; requires a reliable internet connection; more risk of fraud; easier to make errors (e.g. typing in the wrong information); physical cash can't be deposited or withdrawn this way

Expert systems

An expert system mimics human knowledge and experience, using a combination of the two to solve problems or answer questions. Examples include equipment troubleshooting and repair, technical support, and medical diagnosis systems. Expert systems share five key characteristics:

ComponentRole
Knowledge baseA database of facts used to generate rules that solve problems and make decisions
Rule baseA set of rules or logic used to apply the knowledge in the knowledge base to specific problems
Inference engineA program that applies the rules in the rule base to the facts in the knowledge base to solve problems
InterfaceA way for users to interact with the system and provide input
Explanation systemProvides users with explanation and reasoning behind decisions or recommendations, and gives a percentage probability of the accuracy of its conclusions

Case study: movie recommendation system

Inputs: favourite genres/directors; ratings of movies watched (stars, thumbs up/down); previous movie searches (keywords).

Inference engine: matching similar movies to a user's history (based on director, genre, actor, reviews); filtering movies with similar characteristics to those previously watched; filtering movies based on what users with similar tastes have watched or liked.

Output: a personalised list of movie recommendations.

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Consistent results; faster responses; can store large amounts of data; unbiasedOnly as good as the data entered; responses can be cold and lack human emotion; requires training to use correctly

Computers in the retail industry

POS & EFTPOS

Point of Sale (POS) is a computer terminal used to read product barcodes. Barcodes are used in an automatic stock control system in shops. This process involves:

  • Every barcode is assigned to a product in stock
  • Each product record contains its price, stock level, and a description
  • When a barcode is matched with a record, the information is transferred to the POS terminal and the price is displayed on screen
  • The stock level is reduced by one
  • If the current stock level falls below a minimum reorder level, it automatically triggers a restocking process

Electronic Funds Transfer at the Point of Sale (EFTPOS) is how a payment is made using a card or an electronic device such as a smartphone. Examples include Chip and Pin, Contactless, and Near Field Communication (NFC):

Chip and PinContactlessNFC
Enhances security with two-factor authentication; requires customers to enter a PINAllows for faster transactions; enables customers to tap their card on the terminalFacilitates payments through smartphones and other devices; increases convenience for customers

Internet shopping is the act of purchasing goods or services online through a website or mobile app. Online stores are accessible through web browsers, with a wide variety of products available, and are convenient — often open 24/7.

To the business

Recognition systems

A recognition system is a way of using computer technology to automatically identify something. Common examples include facial recognition, speech recognition, and optical character recognition.

OMR

Optical Mark Recognition (OMR) detects marked areas on paper using a special machine to read the marks. Examples include multiple-choice examinations, barcodes, and QR codes — it automates data collection and entry.

OMR — a machine reads which bubble has been marked on each row

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Fast and efficient data collection; reduces manual entry errorsLimited to predetermined responses; sensitive to poor marking or smudging

OCR

Optical Character Recognition (OCR) converts printed or handwritten text into digital format. It's useful for digitising documents, and assists in searching and editing text. Automated Number Plate Recognition (ANPR) is an example of OCR, e.g. reading number plates for controlled entry/exit at airport parking.

AdvantagesDisadvantages
No staff required; faster, no need to stop and take tickets, etc.; stops cars parking illegally, as they cannot enter without authorisationPrivacy concerns, as number plates are recorded; number plates can be cloned; dirty or broken number plates can't be read; lack of staff could lead to vandalism

RFID & NFC

Radio Frequency Identification Devices (RFID) use radio waves to identify and track objects — for example stock tracking, passports, vehicles, and contactless payment. RFID tags are made of a microchip and antenna, and can be passive (uses energy from the reader) or active (has its own power source).

UseDetails
Stock trackingLivestock tracking on a farm; keeping track of animals' location and medical data; active tags used due to the distance involved
PassportsPassive chips in passports; read by a reader to decrypt data stored on the chip, e.g. personal details, photo, fingerprints
VehiclesTracks progress on an assembly line; used on toll roads to automate payment and raise barriers; entry and exit to car parks

Near Field Communication (NFC) is a subset of RFID which allows communication between two devices in very close proximity. NFC can also be passive or active. Smartphones use active NFC to allow contactless payments by tapping the phone on a reader, and can also exchange data with another phone by tapping together (usually back to back).

Satellite systems

GPS

Global Positioning System (GPS) is a satellite system used to track the exact location of an object. It uses orbiting satellites to receive, amplify and transmit signals, using radio frequencies to communicate between satellites and ground stations. GPS requires a direct line of sight.

Case study — sat nav in a car: the position/location of the car is calculated using GPS software; data is transmitted every few seconds; an algorithm calculates the speed/location of the car; the map is updated every few seconds.

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Wide coverage area; real-time data transmission; improved communication in remote locations; accurate location trackingExpensive setup and maintenance; signal interference due to weather or obstacles; limited bandwidth and capacity; privacy concerns and potential for surveillance

GIS

A Geographic Information System (GIS) is a powerful computer system that acts as a database for geographic data. Any data with a location component can be stored, such as physical features (rivers, mountains, roads), man-made features (buildings, utilities like electricity/water/gas), and social/economic data (demographics, crime rates, property value). Two main features of a GIS: analysing geographic data to find patterns/trends/relationships, and visualising geographic data using maps.

Examples of GIS use: assigning the closest emergency services to an emergency; businesses analysing customer demographics, competitors and gaps in the market; tracking deforestation, monitoring pollution, and mapping wildlife habitats.

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Overlaying data based on location reveals hidden patterns; visual representations allow for better decision making; automation; better communication and collaborationCost; only as good as the data it uses; needs specialised training to understand visual elements properly; data privacy concerns

Media communication

Media communication is a system of creating, transmitting, storing and delivering media content using a global network of satellites. Media transmission includes TV signals, radio signals, and internet data. Media communication systems can also act as relays, bouncing signals over a large distance — for example broadcasting live events to a global audience, and ensuring a wider reach to geographically hard-to-reach places.

Worked example4 points

Modern-day drivers rely less on paper maps and more on satellite navigation systems (satnav). Describe the inputs, outputs and processing of a satnav when used by a driver to reach a destination.

Try writing your own answer first — that's how you actually learn it. Don't skip straight to the answer!

Answer (any four of)The destination is input by the driver · the exact position of the vehicle is continually calculated using GPS, using data from 3 or 4 satellites · the on-board computer contains pre-stored road maps · the car's position is displayed on the map/route · an algorithm calculates the route from the current position to the destination, making allowances for traffic jams/roadworks · the car system receives regular updates on traffic conditions · outputs the journey time/ETA/voice directions · outputs speed limits or camera/speed warnings
