Cambridge IGCSE ICT · Theory 5

Effects of Using IT

Microprocessors quietly run a huge amount of modern life — from the heating in your home to the plane overhead — and that brings real trade-offs. This page covers how those devices affect lifestyle, transport and security, plus the health issues that come from spending long hours in front of a screen.

What is a microprocessor?

A microprocessor is an integrated circuit (IC) that contains a central processing unit (CPU). The microprocessor is responsible for executing instructions.

Data security issues

Any device fitted with a microprocessor is open to cybercriminal activity. Microprocessor-controlled devices give hackers more opportunities — for example:

  • Hacking smart home systems to control security devices such as cameras and locks
  • Hacking smart-controlled heating systems to monitor patterns of when heating is on and off — data which can be used to track when people are at home or not

Social interactions

The use of smart devices such as tablets, TVs and phones allows for better communication. There are positive impacts of using technology, such as:

  • Less expensive to stay connected, compared to telephone, post, etc.
  • Easier to make new friends
  • Easier to find people with similar interests

There are also negatives of using technology, such as:

  • Social isolation
  • Increased anxiety from a lack of interaction
  • The way people behave online compared to offline

Monitoring & controlling transport

Microprocessors are used to monitor and control transport systems such as traffic management (e.g. smart motorways), ANPR (Automatic Number Plate Recognition), air traffic control in airports, and railway signalling.

Health issues

Health issues are a common side effect of the prolonged use of IT at home or in the workplace. There are strategies that should be in place to help prevent: repetitive strain injury (RSI), back/neck problems, eye strain, and headaches.

Repetitive strain injury (RSI)

RSI is damage to the fingers, wrists and joints, traditionally caused by prolonged use of a keyboard and mouse.

Effects: pain, numbness, weakness, stiffness.

To eliminate or reduce the risk, a user should:

  • Have the correct posture (angle of arms to the keyboard and mouse)
  • Use a wrist support if necessary
  • Take regular breaks
  • Make use of ergonomic keyboards

Back/neck problems

Back and neck problems are caused by sitting in front of a computer screen for prolonged periods of time.

Effects: pain, stiffness, muscle spasms, weakness, numbness or tingling, sciatica, difficulty sleeping.

To eliminate or reduce the risk, a user should:

  • Use adjustable chairs
  • Take regular breaks
  • Use a foot rest

Eye strain

Eye strain is caused by staring at a computer screen for prolonged periods of time, or by incorrect lighting causing reflections.

Effects: headaches, vision problems (blurred, double, light sensitivity), discomfort.

To eliminate or reduce the risk, a user should:

  • Take regular breaks
  • Use an anti-glare screen filter
  • Change from a CRT to an LCD monitor

Headaches

Headaches are caused by a combination of factors, including poor lighting, flickering screens, reflections, and fatigue.

To eliminate or reduce the risk, a user should:

  • Take regular breaks
  • Have regular eye tests
  • Use an anti-glare screen filter
