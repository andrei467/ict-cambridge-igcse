Part 1 Email

Email is a method of exchanging messages and files over the internet. The main uses of email are personal communication, professional correspondence and marketing. Companies provide guidelines about acceptable use of emails, including the purpose of the email, using acceptable language and email security (including anti-virus software).

Acceptable language guidelines

  • Acceptable and appropriate language must be used depending on the recipient and purpose of the email — e.g. a professional tone for a work-related email
  • No offensive or obscene language or images should be used
  • No racism or violent content should be in email messages
  • No illegal material should be sent
  • The laws within a country can see that emails are monitored and people can be punished for what they write
  • Additional rules: include a clear subject line, use a professional email address, use professional salutations, and do not use all capitals as it implies shouting

Need for security

  • Email security is crucial to protect sensitive information from being accessed or altered
  • Email inboxes should be protected by strong passwords which are frequently changed
  • Spam filters and antivirus software should be in place at all times to protect users’ inboxes from both passive attacks and active attacks

Netiquette

Netiquette (Internet etiquette) is the need to respect others online. It is an agreement to respect the opinion of others in online forums, internet messages and emails. Common examples:

  • Most posts are public and can potentially be read by anyone
  • A clear, inoffensive and succinct language will be used
  • Give a good impression of yourself, considering the tone of writing, spelling, punctuation and grammar

Email groups

  • Email groups allow for mass communication with a defined set of recipients
  • It is easier to send out multiple emails if the addresses are grouped together
  • Benefit: it ensures no one misses out on an email containing key information
  • Drawback: spammers can utilise them to target a large group of people with ease

Spam

Spam is unwanted/unsolicited junk mail which is sent in large numbers to inboxes. It fills users’ inboxes, often with advertising content or for phishing/spreading malware. Phishing emails act as a reputable company or organisation in the aim of getting your personal details such as usernames and passwords.

How can spam emails be detected?

  • Often has multiple spelling or grammatical errors
  • They often use broken English, though this is now improving with the use of AI
  • Spam emails will not address you by name
  • Often ask you to enter your details by clicking a link — reputable companies have now stopped doing this to make identifying spam a lot easier

How can spam emails be prevented?

  • Email providers’ anti-spam filters become stronger and more efficient
  • Users can ensure they do not agree to marketing emails from companies they register with
  • Users can also ensure they do not reply to emails with sensitive data or complete online forms by clicking links in an email — instead, they should visit the website of their own accord and then log in as usual

Part 2 The Internet

The Internet is a network of networks which allows users to share and distribute information outside of an organisation and to send and receive emails. It has many features users can use to communicate and share information with others.

Social networks

A social network website is a platform where users can connect with others and share content, e.g. Facebook, TikTok, X (Twitter), Instagram and LinkedIn. Users usually create a profile and can share text, images, videos and links. They facilitate interaction, collaboration and information sharing on a large scale, and privacy settings allow users to control who can see their content.

ISPs, web browsers & URLs

  • An Internet Service Provider (ISP) is a company that provides access to the Internet to users and businesses, by leasing equipment and telecommunication lines to users
  • A web browser is a software application used to locate, retrieve and display content on the WWW (web pages with images, videos and other files)
  • A hyperlink is a word/phrase/image which references data the reader can follow by clicking or tapping, usually taking you to another web page
  • Most browsers have a home page, the ability to bookmark favourites, a history of visited websites and hyperlinks to navigate between pages
  • A Uniform Resource Locator (URL) is the web address a user types into a web browser. Websites are stored as an IP address, which is not user friendly, so an alphanumeric format is used for humans, e.g. www.savemyexams.com

Search engines

Search engines are tools that locate and display web pages related to the search terms entered by the user. They index millions of web pages and use algorithms to rank the relevance of each page. They work in three stages:

StageWhat happens
CrawlingWeb crawlers scour the internet daily to retrieve new websites
IndexingWebsites are categorised based on the content of their web pages, keywords and metadata
RankingWebsites are ranked and listed on search engine pages; this depends on many factors to display the most relevant results
  • Search engines can give an overwhelming amount of information, so use specific and relevant search terms. Quotation marks are for exact phrases, plus signs for mandatory terms and minus signs for excluding terms
  • Relevance is decided by the search engine’s algorithm (keyword frequency, page quality and EEAT). Reliable information typically comes from educational establishments, governments or well-established industry websites

Evaluating information found on the internet

  • Not all information is accurate or reliable
  • Assess reliability by considering the reputation and credibility of the source
  • Determine validity by checking it against other reputable sources
  • Consider whether the information is biased, promoting a particular viewpoint
  • Check how up-to-date the information is — outdated information can be misleading

Part 3 Internet protocols

A protocol is a set of rules that govern communication on a network. There are protocols for different purposes, including HTTP & HTTPS, FTP, SSL & TLS, VoIP and SMTP.

HTTP & HTTPS

  • Hypertext Transfer Protocol (HTTP) allows communication between clients and servers for website viewing
  • HTTP allows clients to receive data from the server (fetching a webpage) and send data to the server (submitting a form, uploading a file)
  • HTTPS works in the same way as HTTP but with an added layer of security. All data sent and received using HTTPS is encrypted
  • HTTPS is used to protect sensitive information such as passwords, financial information and personal data

FTP

  • File Transfer Protocol (FTP) allows sending and receiving files between computers
  • Uploading and downloading files to/from a web server is often completed using FTP
  • FTP offers greater efficiency and support for bulk transfers and large files, such as resuming interrupted transfers
  • FTP clients are software applications that use the FTP protocol to make the process easier for users

SSL & TLS

  • Secure Socket Layer (SSL) is a security protocol developed to provide secure communication over the internet
  • TLS is a successor to SSL and is also a security protocol used to provide secure communication over the internet
  • They both use a combination of symmetric and asymmetric encryption to secure data and ensure data integrity
  • SSL operates by encrypting a user’s data using a public key. This is done by sending a digital certificate to the user’s browser, which contains the public key that can be used for authentication

Where is SSL used? Wherever a secure connection is required, for example:

  • Online banking
  • Online shopping
  • Using cloud storage
  • Messaging
  • Social networking websites
  • Intranets/extranets
